\chapter*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}

This internship set out to turn an idle physical server into a secure, durable web hosting platform, capable of hosting several applications at once. At the time of writing, the foundations described in this report are in place and validated in production: authentication, HTTPS adapted to a genuinely constrained network, a correctly scoped firewall, key-only SSH access on both virtual machines, continuous integration with tested and verified deployments, and full-stack supervision. Two applications, the supervision dashboard and GitLab, are published under the domain \texttt{ucac-icam.info} through one named tunnel per machine, without a single port opened on the network. What remains is less about acquiring new skills than about finishing and protecting what was built: making the platform come back by itself after a power interruption, putting an access-control layer in front of the published services, and hosting a third application.

Beyond the technical deliverable, this internship was also a first sustained exposure to how infrastructure work actually unfolds: initial plans (a directly exposed reverse proxy, for instance) meeting a constraint discovered only in practice (CGNAT) and having to be reworked without losing the security guarantees already built, rather than a specification followed from a clean, known-in-advance starting point. Diagnosing failures methodically, distinguishing a configured state from an actually running one, re-auditing a service when its exposure changes, and documenting decisions as they were made rather than after the fact are the habits I expect to carry furthest beyond this specific project.
